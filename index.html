Transverse Mercator (TM) and Lambert Conformal Conic (LCC) Projection Distortion Explainer/Visualizer

This article presents and explains distortion factor introduced by map projection and its pattern focused over territory of Nepal.
A map projection introduces distortion in an attempt to convert or transform curved earth surface (sphere or ellipsoid) into flat paper (2D rectangular plane).
Two major projection system - Transverse Mercator (TM) and Lambert Conformal Conic (LCC) are presented here, specifically focused to distortion pattern caused by them.
TM is cylindrical, conformal, and secant. LCC is conical, conformal, and secant.
Recall your basics of TM and LCC. Recall your experiences and practices of using TM (in form of UTM or MUTM) and LCC while doing your surveying, mapping, GIS, and geomatics projects.
The article progresses from information on UTM to MUTM to LCC. Specifically focused on distortion factor.
The concept of blockwise distortion factor and arc-length (curved distance) are additional items at the end of page.

Because of its minimal distortion and better than UTM, MUTM has been adopted /applied in large scale mapping (e.g. 1:500, 1:1250, 1:2500, 1:10000). Cadastral mapping need to be done at large scale, this is the reason why MUTM projection has been adopted.
The problem is, because single MUTM zone is just 3 degree width, it doesn't cover the entire east-west extent of Nepal. Thus, we introduced 3 different MUTM zones.

3 Modified UTM zones are required and suffice to cover entire territory of Nepal. As Modified UTM provides minimal distortion compared to UTM, why not use 3 separate MUTM zones for survyeing and mapping purpose in Nepal. 3 MUTM zones put together, covers the extent of Nepal, lets call each MUTM81 (MUTM zone having 81 deg E longitude as central meridian), MUTM84, and MUTM87. MUTM81 covers western part, MUTM84 covers central part, and MUTM87 covers eastern part of Nepal.
Then we naturally ask ourselves, if my surveying and mapping area is Jhapa district, I would use MUTM87 zone for projection, but what if my project area lies at the boundary of these zones, e.g. at the boundaries of MUTM84 and MUTM87? For example, Sindhuli and Kavrepalanchok district falls at both MUTM84 and MUTM87 zone.
Also, we come to ask, if my project area covers entire province (provincial level), which might extend to coverage of two neighboring MUTM zones, then, what we are gonna do?
The advantage is MUTM has less distortion, suits for large scale mapping and covers only relatively smaller geographic coverage. The disadvantage is one MUTM zone doesnt't cover area that extends beyond 3 degree width in longitude. That is why mutliple MUTM zones are introduced in a first place. The project area that lies at boundary of MUTM zones or the project area that span beyond the width of single MUTM zone, the diffculty arise in such scenario.
So, which projection system will I use when I am doing province-wide GIS analysis, nation-wide GIS analysis? Lets say nationwide landuse mapping, nationwide vegetation coverage or forestry, nationwide topograpic features overlay with other thematic information?
These provice-wide / nation-wide examples, just mentioned here, are small scale mapping. The small scale (e.g. 1:25,000 or 1:100,000) absorbs the distortion error (the amount of distortion introduced due to projection). Now the distortion ceases to be key or critical factor at these kind of GIS analysis or scenario.
. In these cases, we again move from MUTM to UTM projection system (however UTM has limitation of being just 6 degree width) or any other projection system that solves our problem, that fits our purpose.
By now, we realized, we have been using UTM, MUTM and multiple MUTMs together for our surveying, mapping, GIS, and geomatics purposes. Maybe we need a third kind of projection, a next or newer class of projection!.

Lambert Conformal Conic (LCC) is conformal, conical, and secant projection. In LCC parameters shown in above Fig 4, the location of standard parallel is according to proposed by Suresh Man Shrestha (SMS). The location of SP is key to distortion factor and pattern. This cone touches or intersects the earth (sphere or ellipsoid) along two parallels (parallels of latitude). Such parallel is standard parallel. These intersecting parallel are also secant lines. There is no distortion introduced at standard parallels (the scale factor is unity here).
Double standard parallel LCC (2sp LCC), the LCC which we introduced here is called double standard parallel LCC. If a cone is made to touch at only single parallel of latitude, then that is called single standard parallel LCC (1sp LCC). Here, we explore 2sp LCC.
The location of standard parallel is the key decision, that further or later governs the distortion amount and distortion pattern.
Inspect the above Fig 4. Border parallels (solid red line) are southern-most and northern-most bound. Standard parallels are dotted blue line. Notice lower sp is at 27deg N latitude and upper sp is at 29.75deg N latitude.
Now, we can inspect the distortion and its pattern. The blue text is the distortion value, red text is distortion error (scale error), and lightgray text is distortion introduced per km. One clear pattern is no distortion introduced at standard parallel, the distortion goes increasing if you move inwards from standard parallel, keeps increasing, and reaches maximum at certain latitude. Another clear pattern is, take lower standard parallel, there is no distortion at this sp (scale factor of unity), if you move upwards (inwards), the distortion keeps increasing, reaches to maximum at some point (around mid of both sp), then the distortion begins to decrease and reaches to no distortion (scale factor of unity) at upper sp.
Also, take both sp and move inwards from both sp, you find distortion keeps increasing and reaches the maximum at certain point or latitude.
Take any one of the standard parallel and move outwards, you find after certain point there is a dramatic increment in distortion compared to inward directions from both standard parallel.
These two standard parallels are put in optimal location, resulting the minimal distortion we can achieve. But before concluding and settling for these two standard parallel positions, lets see few variations in LCC, different than this one. Continue below for other variations and experiment of LCC.

For LCC system, the key parameter to distortion and its pattern is the location of SP. What if we change the location of SP than earlier settings (Fig 4). For convenience, because the LCC parameters (location of SP, origin of latitude and longitude, false easting and northing) used in Fig 4, were proposed by Suresh Man Shrestha (SMS), lets call it SMS LCC. Whenever you see SMS LCC, this means Fig 4 parameters. In this article, we only focus and discuss the SPs and its location. We will not discuss other LCC parameters such as origin of latitude and longitude and false easting and northing.
Lets change SP to 27.5deg N and 29.5deg N latitude. Would we achieve minimal distortion? Inspect Fig 5 above and compare with Fig 4. We squeezed the inner space between SPs in this case compared to SMS LCC. The maximum distortion inside SPs is better or less than that compared to SMS LCC. We achieved minimal distortion inside. However, notice the dramatic increase in distortion factor while moving outward from both SPs. At extreme border parallel, at 26deg N latitude, the distortion introduced is around 80cm in 1 km and at 30.5deg N latitude, the distortion introduced is around 45cm in 1 km.
In case of SMS LCC, the distortion introduced is 56cm and 40cm in 1km at 26deg N (southern-most border parallel) and 30.5deg N (northern-most border parallel) latitude.
Thus, we have evaluated /analyzed that SMS LCC provides better distortion pattern than this one in overall, taking entire extent of Nepal territory.
This case clearly shows that SMS LCC performs best or optimal than any other variations of LCC. We can change the location of SP anywhere we like and analyze the distortion pattern as an experiment.

Having discussed UTM, MUTM and LCC, we know and we saw that distortion factor varies at every point. The TM distortion factor varies in lateral direction (longitudinal direction along the parellel) and varies in north-south direction (latitudinal direction along the meridians or longitude line) for LCC.
Thus, we ask:

  • If distortion varies at every point, what distortion factor (scale factor) will I apply for my traverese leg or GNSS baseline or (any length/distance)?.
  • If distortion varies, then what distortion factor (a single value) will I apply in surveying and mapping area of my project?

Because distortion varies at every point, it would be wise and managable, if we apply single scale factor (a single value: either average or central or any approach that gives us single value) for reasonably larger coverage block, within which that single value suffice as scale factor, is the concept of blockwise scale factor.
Fig 7 shows the blockwise scale factor for LCC.
The block shown here is 0.5deg by 0.5deg. What should be the size of block? The mentioned scale factor is not actual scale factor computed at centroid or central point of each block. But it is the line scale factor. Point scale factor is term that denotes the scale factor computed at that particual point. We were talking about point scale factor until now in this article since the beginning. The term Line scale factor denotes the scale factor applied to any particular line segment. Take the point scale factor of both end points of that line and compute the mean value, that is the Line scale factor for that line. Beside mean value, the precise way of computing line scale factor is K = (k1+k2+4km)/6). k1, k2 is point scale factor of endpoints and km is mean of k1 and k2.
Lets inspect the blockwise scale factor in Fig 7. For blocks that are placed /aligned in lateral direction, the distortion factor doesn't change, and that is the nature of LCC. For blocks in north-south direction, blockwise distortion factor changes, but it changes gradually which introduces negligible effect as a change of factor. Even if your project area covers blocks of extreme ends (the northernmost block and southernmost block), the difference in distortion factor is negligible enough.

Distortion is introduced invariably, if I want my curved length (arc length) computed over reference ellipsoid to be converted to equivalent distance in grid plane. Converting curved length on reference ellipsoid surface to equivalent length on MUTM plane or LCC plane, this is what projection formula does at the cost of distortion. We want both the minimal distortion and the conveninece. Which projection system, MUTM or LCC, does solve my problem?
